# Early Risk Response Procedure

## Purpose

To verify an emerging customer risk, establish ownership, communicate before uncertainty grows, and confirm recovery against the customer's workflow.

This procedure is intentionally adaptable. Response windows, escalation levels, and internal roles should be aligned with the organization using it.

## Trigger

Start the procedure when one or more of these signals appears:

- an important customer commitment is missed or likely to be missed;
- a milestone or adoption pattern is worsening;
- the same service or workflow issue repeats;
- an important stakeholder disengages or changes;
- a customer workaround becomes part of normal operations;
- an escalation remains open without a clear next decision; or
- negative feedback is linked to an operational consequence.

## Required inputs

- original customer message or source record;
- recent account history;
- affected workflow and stakeholder;
- known open commitments;
- available usage, service, or outcome evidence; and
- current internal owner, if one exists.

## Procedure

### 1. Verify the signal

- Return to the source rather than relying on a summary.
- Confirm whether the event is isolated, repeated, or worsening.
- Separate fact, interpretation, and missing information.

**Output:** a one-sentence verified signal and an evidence link.

### 2. Define the customer consequence

- Identify the workflow, commitment, or outcome affected.
- Ask what becomes harder if the issue continues.
- Record the stakeholder who carries the consequence.

**Output:** a plain-language consequence statement.

### 3. Set the response level

| Condition | Response level |
| --- | --- |
| Material consequence and difficult recovery | Immediate coordinated response |
| Material consequence but incomplete evidence | Urgent verification with proactive customer update |
| Repeated friction with manageable consequence | Planned corrective action |
| Weak evidence and no confirmed consequence | Monitor with a defined discovery action |

### 4. Assign one accountable owner

Name the person responsible for moving the risk to the next decision. Supporting teams can own tasks, but the customer-facing risk should not become ownerless between functions.

**Output:** owner, action, next update point, and expected closure evidence.

### 5. Communicate before full resolution

A useful update contains four parts:

1. what was observed;
2. why it matters to the customer's workflow;
3. what is being done next; and
4. when the customer will receive another update.

Do not overstate certainty. If verification is still in progress, say so clearly.

### 6. Track the corrective action

- Keep customer-facing and internal actions visible in one record.
- Escalate when the agreed update point is missed, evidence worsens, or recovery becomes less reversible.
- Record decisions, not only meetings and messages.

### 7. Validate recovery

Closure requires evidence that the original workflow is reliable again or that the customer has accepted a clear alternative.

Ask:

- Did the signal improve?
- Did the customer consequence reduce?
- Is any workaround still required?
- Does training, documentation, configuration, process, or product context need to change?

## Closure checklist

- [ ] Original signal verified
- [ ] Customer consequence documented
- [ ] Accountable owner named
- [ ] Customer received clear updates
- [ ] Corrective action completed
- [ ] Recovery checked against the workflow
- [ ] Reusable learning documented

## AI checkpoint

If AI was used to summarize history or identify a pattern:

- [ ] the source records were reviewed;
- [ ] contradictory evidence was checked;
- [ ] sensitive information was removed;
- [ ] the recommendation was validated by a person; and
- [ ] no customer commitment was created without owner approval.
