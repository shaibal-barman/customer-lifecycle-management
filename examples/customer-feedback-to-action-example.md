# Worked Example: Customer Feedback to Action

> This is a generalized example based on recurring situations across customer service, digital delivery, training, and product improvement. It is not a record of one specific customer, and it contains no confidential information.

## Initial feedback

> “The workflow is unreliable, so our team keeps using a manual process.”

The statement matters, but it is not yet actionable. It does not identify which users are affected, where the workflow fails, how often the manual process is used, or whether the cause belongs to product, service, process, or understanding.

## 1. Reconstruct the situation

The account owner asks for evidence around five questions:

1. Which role is trying to complete the workflow?
2. What outcome is that person responsible for?
3. At which step do they leave the intended process?
4. What do they do manually instead?
5. What operational consequence follows?

The review shows that users can complete the normal path, but they leave the system when an exception occurs. Different locations handle that exception differently, and no single team owns the handoff.

## 2. Classify before assigning action

| Area | Evidence | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product or configuration | The exception path is difficult to recognize | Review the workflow and configuration with Product and QA |
| Service | Follow-up varies by location | Define one response and update rhythm |
| Process | The handoff has no consistent owner | Assign ownership for the exception |
| Understanding | Training covered only the normal path | Add role-based practice for the exception |

The feedback is not forced into a single category. More than one condition can be true, but every action still needs a named owner and supporting evidence.

## 3. Create the action record

| Action | Owner | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm the intended exception workflow | Account owner and customer stakeholder | Agreed workflow and responsibility |
| Review configuration and reproduce the issue | Product, Engineering, or QA | Documented finding and next decision |
| Standardize the handoff | Operations | One visible owner and update point |
| Deliver focused enablement | Customer Success | Users complete the normal and exception paths |
| Review adoption after the change | Account owner | Manual workaround reduces and intended completion improves |

## 4. Close the loop with the customer

The customer does not need an internal activity list. The useful update is:

- what the team understood from the feedback;
- which part is confirmed and which part is still being checked;
- what will change in the workflow, service, or enablement;
- who owns each commitment; and
- what evidence will be reviewed afterward.

## 5. Confirm whether value improved

Success is not “feedback logged” or “training delivered.” The relevant evidence is whether:

- the intended workflow is completed more consistently;
- the exception has a clear owner;
- the manual workaround is used less often;
- users know what to do when the normal path fails; and
- the customer sees a practical improvement in visibility, time, or reliability.

This example shows the difference between recording a customer opinion and making it useful. Voice of Customer creates value only when another team can verify the situation, make a decision, own an action, and confirm the effect with the customer.
