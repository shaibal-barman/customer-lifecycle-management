# 3. Adoption and Enablement

Training is successful when the customer can complete an important task with less uncertainty—not when every feature has been demonstrated.

## Design from the task backward

Before preparing a session, define:

1. **Role:** Who is performing the work?
2. **Task:** What must they complete?
3. **Friction:** Where do they hesitate, fail, or depend on another person?
4. **Exception:** What should happen when the normal path does not work?
5. **Evidence:** What will show that the workflow is being used correctly afterward?

## A focused enablement session

For a short working session, I use this sequence:

| Stage | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Confirm the outcome | Restate the task and why it matters to the customer's operation |
| Show the normal path | Demonstrate only the steps needed for the real workflow |
| Let the customer perform it | Observe where the user pauses or chooses the wrong path |
| Practice one exception | Cover the most likely failure or escalation route |
| Confirm evidence and next step | Agree on what will be checked after the session and who will follow up |

The customer should do more of the work than the presenter. Observation during practice often reveals a configuration, permission, terminology, or process gap that a feature tour would miss.

## Adoption evidence ladder

Adoption should be assessed in stages:

1. **Access:** The correct people can reach what they need.
2. **Completion:** They can finish the required workflow.
3. **Consistency:** The workflow is repeated without avoidable support.
4. **Operational effect:** Completion improves time, quality, visibility, or reliability.
5. **Business evidence:** The customer can connect that operational effect to an outcome they value.

High login volume can coexist with weak adoption. The strongest evidence is reliable completion of the workflow that matters.

## Follow-up rule

Every enablement activity should end with:

- one behavior to observe;
- one evidence source;
- one owner;
- one review point; and
- one action if adoption does not improve.

Without that follow-up, training becomes an event rather than a customer outcome.
