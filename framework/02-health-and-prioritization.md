# 2. Account Health and Prioritization

A scaled portfolio cannot receive equal time. It should receive consistent logic.

Health is not a color selected from memory. It is a short explanation of what is happening, why it matters, and how confident we are in the evidence.

## Five evidence areas

| Evidence area | Healthy evidence | Emerging risk evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome progress | Agreed milestone or business result is moving | Milestone is stalled, unclear, or no longer relevant |
| Workflow adoption | Key roles complete the intended workflow | Usage exists but the important workflow is avoided or incomplete |
| Service reliability | Important commitments are consistently met | Repeated exception, unresolved issue, or unstable handoff |
| Relationship coverage | Active contact with users and decision-makers | Single-threaded relationship, stakeholder silence, or sponsor change |
| Open obligations | Actions have owners, dates, and visible progress | Important promise is overdue, ownerless, or repeatedly moved |

## Record three things for every signal

1. **Evidence:** What was observed? Link to the source where possible.
2. **Direction:** Is it improving, stable, or worsening?
3. **Confidence:** Is the conclusion supported, partial, or still uncertain?

A health score without these three fields is difficult to defend and easy to misuse.

## Priority is a decision, not a score

I prioritize by asking three questions:

- **Consequence:** What customer or business outcome is affected?
- **Time sensitivity:** Does delay reduce the chance of recovery?
- **Reversibility:** Can the situation be corrected easily later, or is the effect difficult to undo?

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| High consequence and difficult to reverse | Act immediately; set an internal owner and customer update point |
| High consequence but evidence is incomplete | Verify urgently and communicate what is being checked |
| Repeated friction with limited immediate impact | Plan enablement, workflow correction, or product investigation |
| Isolated signal with no verified consequence | Monitor and gather context before escalating |

## Portfolio review rhythm

### Daily: exceptions

- overdue customer commitments;
- new operational failures;
- worsening adoption or engagement signals;
- unresolved escalations; and
- decisions blocked by missing ownership.

### Weekly: patterns

- accounts with repeated friction;
- common enablement gaps;
- feedback appearing across customers;
- actions that keep moving without closure; and
- accounts where health confidence is low.

### Monthly: value evidence

- customer outcomes demonstrated;
- adoption linked to an operational result;
- recurring risk reduced or removed;
- stakeholder alignment; and
- next value milestone.

## The minimum account decision record

Every prioritized account should have:

- the verified signal;
- the customer consequence;
- the next action;
- one accountable owner;
- the next update point; and
- the evidence required to close the risk.

This record keeps prioritization explainable when the portfolio changes quickly.
